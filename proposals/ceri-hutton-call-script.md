## THE REFERRAL PLAY

### The Offer to Ceri

Don't frame this as a "commission." Frame it as **"I want to make it worth your time when you connect me with the right studios."**

**Structure:**
- $100 referral fee per studio that becomes a paying client
- Paid in batches of 5 ($500 drops at a time)
- Triggered after 3rd converted client (so you're not paying out on the first couple while you build the relationship)
- No limit on referrals — the more he sends, the more he earns
- Paid quarterly or on hitting 5-batch milestones

**How to say it:**
> "Look, if you know studios that have budget and need UA or creator campaigns — send them my way. After the first 3 that convert, I'll do $100 per referral, paid out in $500 batches every time you hit 5. It's a small thing but I want you to know that when you connect me with the right people, there's something in it for you beyond goodwill."

**Why this works:**
- Low enough that it doesn't feel transactional or sleazy
- Batch payment means he has to stack referrals (stays engaged long-term)
- "After 3" means you're not paying out on test referrals — only once the pipeline proves real
- Makes him a scout, not just a connector — he's motivated to find studios with actual budget

### The Real Incentive (Beyond Cash)

The $100 is the surface play. The deeper incentive for Ceri:
- **His studios succeed** → he looks good → his advisory business grows
- If he recommends UA/attribution and his clients *actually* get results, that validates HIS advisory work
- He can tell future clients "I have a UA/attribution partner I work with" = more comprehensive offering
- Over time, you become embedded in his recommendation stack — every funded studio he touches hears your name

## THE THESIS (What You Believe — Know This Cold)

**One sentence:** "Gambling operators know exactly what a player is worth before they open the doors. We give game studios the same clarity — without the extractive mechanics. That's what makes gaming investable."

**The problem:** Australian games funding has zero post-release accountability. You can receive $100K from Screen Australia, release a game that earns $0, and successfully acquit the grant. The system measures *who* gets funded (gender diversity KPIs, residency requirements) — not *what* gets achieved (revenue, installs, retention, jobs sustained).

**The difference between gaming and gambling in government's eyes:** attitude to ROI. Gambling proves revenue in real-time before asking for anything. Gaming asks for money with a "cultural milestone" 18 months later and no commercial plan required. Bridge that data gap and gaming becomes as investable as any other entertainment sector.

**What we build:** Real-time attribution infrastructure. Creator-driven UA. Install/revenue/retention tracking. The data layer that lets studios prove their game works, lets government prove their investment returned value, and lets the whole ecosystem attract more funding.

**The punchline:** Every one succeeded through organic/creator discovery. None had a funded UA or marketing plan. Imagine what happens when quality games get intentional, attributed distribution.

### The Other 98% (The Problem)
- 14,000+ games launch on Steam every year
- 98% are indie
- **50% earn less than $500 lifetime.** Not monthly. Total. Forever.
- $4.2 billion AU consumer games spending in 2025 — but AU dev studios only captured $226M of that
- 80% of AU dev income comes from overseas sales — but nobody tracks which funded games contributed

### Funding Reality
- Screen Australia: $100K grants, ~26 games per round (~$2.6M/round)
- Screen QLD: Up to $300K lifetime per project
- NSW: 10% Digital Games Rebate on $350K+ spend
- Travel: $2-7.5K per dev to attend PAX/GDC/Gamescom — zero outcomes tracking

### What Screen Agencies Report As Success
- Gender diversity KPIs (58% women/non-binary in creative roles)
- Production expenditure ("$1.7B spent on drama")
- Number of projects funded
- "Reaching a significant milestone" (release = success regardless of revenue)
- **They do NOT track:** revenue, installs, retention, employment sustained, export value, ROI

### Phase 2: THE OBSERVATION (5 min)

Frame it as a question, not a lecture:

"So here's what I'm seeing — tell me if this resonates or if I'm off base. The screen agencies are doing important work funding games. But from what I can tell, there's basically zero requirement to track what happens after a game ships. You can get $100K from Screen Australia, release a game that nobody plays, and the grant is acquitted because you 'reached a significant milestone.'

Meanwhile in gambling, every single dollar is tracked in real-time before an operator even opens their doors. Government understands gambling's economic contribution precisely. With gaming... it's vibes. 'We funded 26 games.' But did they make money? Did they create sustained jobs? Did they export? Nobody knows.

And then when someone in treasury asks 'why should we increase the games budget?' — there's no data to answer with. That's how the AIGF got killed in 2014. $20M fund, axed because nobody could prove it worked."

**Then ask:** "Does that match what you're seeing? What's your take on how this gets solved?"

### Phase 4: YOUR SOLUTION (5 min)

Only go here if Phase 3 showed genuine interest:

"So what we're building is the layer that sits between the studio and the market — specifically around creators and UA. Here's how it could work in a funded context:

1. **Studio gets $100K grant.** Part of the deliverable includes implementing our attribution SDK + creator strategy.
2. **Creators drive discovery.** Matched by genre, audience, market. Tracked with MMP-verified attribution.
3. **Real-time data flows.** Installs, revenue, retention, engagement — available to the studio AND optionally to the funding body.
4. **Acquittal becomes evidence-based.** Instead of 'we released it' → it's '$100K generated 50,000 installs, $400K revenue, sustained 3 jobs for 18 months.'
5. **The flywheel:** More data → better funding decisions → more successful games → more funding justified → bigger ecosystem.

The closest analogy: we're the AppsFlyer/Adjust of creator-driven gaming UA. But purpose-built for the AU indie ecosystem and positioned to solve the accountability gap that killed the AIGF."
