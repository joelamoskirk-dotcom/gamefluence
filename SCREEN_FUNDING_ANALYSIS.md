# Australian Games Funding: Cost-Benefit Analysis & The Case for Real-Time Attribution

## Internal Reference Document — For Ceri Hutton call, investor conversations, government pitches.

> **Framing note:** This is NOT an attack on screen agencies. They do important work enabling Australian stories. The argument is: real-time analytics is the missing infrastructure that would make their investments more defensible, increase the total funding pool, and evolve the ecosystem from "fund the launch" to "fund the economy." The difference between gaming and gambling in government's eyes is the attitude to ROI *before* anything is created — gambling proves revenue first, gaming asks for funding without a commercial plan. We bridge that gap.

## 1. HOW SCREEN AGENCIES CURRENTLY REPORT SUCCESS

### What They Measure (Input/Process Metrics)
- **Gender diversity KPIs**: "58% of key creative roles held by women/non-binary people" (2024/25)
- **Total expenditure**: "$1.7 billion spent on drama production" (23/24) — they report what was *spent*, not what was *returned*
- **Projects funded**: "26 games funded this round" — counting outputs, not outcomes
- **Cultural milestones**: Release, early access, or "cultural worth" = acquittal success

### What They DON'T Measure (Outcome Metrics)
- Revenue generated post-release
- Downloads / installs / player count
- Player retention (D1, D7, D30)
- Employment sustained 12+ months post-funding
- Export revenue generated
- Return on taxpayer investment
- Whether anyone actually played the game
- Cost-per-acquisition of any audience

### The KPI Problem
Screen Australia's only public KPI related to *commercial outcomes* is: "take the game to a significant milestone of its development." That milestone can be release, early access, or "a quality suitable to submission for an accolade signifying cultural worth."

**Translation:** You can receive $100K, release a game that earns $0, and successfully acquit the grant. The system has no commercial accountability.

## 3. TRAVEL FUNDING: THE "WHO NOT WHAT" PROBLEM

### What's Being Funded
State agencies fund developers to travel to events:
- **Screen NSW**: PAX Market Support ($2,150/dev), GDC Market Travel, Gamescom Market Travel, GCAP Travel Support, BitSummit
- **Screen Queensland**: GDC grants ($3-5K), Gamescom grants ($4-7.5K each), BitSummit ($3K per project)
- **Multiple states**: All run variations of "send developers to events" programs

### The Structure
- Typical grant: $2,000-$7,500 per developer/team for travel + accommodation + booth
- Typical cohort: 5-10 developers per event per state
- Events covered: PAX AU, GDC (San Francisco), Gamescom (Cologne), GCAP, Tokyo Game Show, BitSummit

### What Success Looks Like To Them
From Screen NSW: developers gain "experience and make new connections"
From Screen QLD: developers "attract support and finance for their projects"

### What's NOT Tracked
- Did anyone at GDC actually sign a publishing deal as a result?
- Did the PAX booth generate wishlists? How many? Did those convert to sales?
- Did the Gamescom trip result in any revenue-generating partnership?
- 12 months later, did the funded developer release a commercially successful game?
- What was the ROI per dollar of travel funding?

### The Pattern
Travel funding is allocated based on **who applies and meets residency requirements** — not based on commercial readiness, market viability, or likelihood of generating measurable outcomes. A developer with a polished, market-ready game and a clear commercial strategy gets the same $2,150 as a developer with a prototype and no go-to-market plan.

**This is the "who not what" problem:** funding flows based on identity (NSW-based, emerging, diverse) rather than outcome potential (market-ready, commercially viable, measurable ROI pathway).

## 4. GAMING vs GAMBLING: THE ATTITUDE TO ROI

### Why Government Funds Gambling Infrastructure Happily
- Real-time data: every dollar tracked, every player profiled, every session measured
- Revenue projections BEFORE investment: gambling operators present detailed financial models
- Ongoing reporting: daily/weekly/monthly revenue, tax contribution, employment data
- Clear ROI story: "$X invested → $Y in tax revenue → Z jobs sustained"
- The data exists to defend the investment to treasury

### Why Government Under-Funds Gaming
- No real-time data: nobody knows if funded games generate revenue
- No revenue projections required: grants assessed on "cultural worth" and "significant milestone"
- No ongoing reporting: once the game ships, the relationship ends
- No ROI story: "$100K invested → game released → ???"
- The data to defend the investment DOESN'T EXIST

### The Gap We Fill
We don't need gaming to adopt gambling's extractive mechanics. We need gaming to adopt gambling's **accountability infrastructure**:
- Real-time install and revenue tracking (the data output)
- Attribution per marketing dollar spent (where revenue comes from)
- Retention curves (whether players stick around)
- Lifetime value modelling (what a player is worth over time)
- Commercial plan requirements BEFORE funding (what are we trying to achieve)

**This is the core thesis:** The difference between a gambler and a gamer in government's eyes isn't the product — it's the data. Gambling proves its value in real-time. Gaming asks for money and provides a "cultural milestone" 18 months later. Bridge that data gap and gaming becomes as investable as any other entertainment sector.

## 6. WHAT REAL-TIME ANALYTICS CHANGES

### For Funded Studios
- Know within weeks (not years) if their game is working commercially
- Optimise spend in-flight rather than discovering failure post-mortem
- Build a case for follow-on funding based on traction data
- Prove their grant generated economic output for acquittal

### For Screen Agencies
- Track portfolio performance across all funded titles
- Identify which types of games/teams/genres generate best ROI
- Present treasury with annual economic impact data
- Justify budget increases with evidence, not anecdote
- Learn what makes funded games succeed vs fail — and adjust criteria

### For The Ecosystem
- Intelligence flows back: what genres work, what markets respond, what price points convert
- The industry professionalises: commercial plans become standard, not optional
- More funding flows in: because the accountability exists to justify it
- "Fund the economy" replaces "fund the launch": ongoing live-ops measurement proves sustained value

## 7. REFERENCE CASES (for future conversations)

### AIGF Death (2014) — The Cautionary Tale
- $20M fund, est. 2012. Abbott government axed it in 2014 budget.
- Only one distribution round completed: 21 projects, $2.6M
- No public outcomes data was EVER published on those 21 projects
- Government argument: "Can't justify the spend"
- **Lesson:** Without attribution, funding is politically indefensible

### Victorian Commonwealth Games ($589M waste, 2024)
- Cost blew from $2.5B to $7B with no measurement catching it
- Auditor General: "significant waste of taxpayer money" with "no discernible benefit"
- Parliamentary inquiry blamed "grossly underestimated costs" and lack of frank advice
- **Lesson:** Investment without real-time measurement = uncontrolled risk

### Massive Monster (Cult of the Lamb) — The Positive Model
- Melbourne studio, supported through Film Victoria ecosystem
- $90M+ revenue, 4.5M copies, BAFTA nominated
- Founders now running MASS co-working space + private investment fund for local devs
- **Lesson:** When funded studios succeed, they reinvest in the ecosystem. But we need to be able to identify and support the NEXT Massive Monster BEFORE they hit — not after.

### Team Cherry (Hollow Knight) — Self-Funded Success
- 3-person team, Adelaide. Kickstarter funded ($57K).
- $316M gross revenue on Steam alone. Silksong: $100M in first months.
- Zero government funding involvement.
- **Lesson:** The biggest Australian game export ever received no government support. If attribution existed during their growth phase, government would have had the data to offer proactive support and claim credit for enabling the ecosystem.
